# Decision Log

Format: **ID — Title** · Status · Date. Reason in one or two lines.

## Open (need your call)

- **D-001 — Product name: Openpolis.** Decided 2026-11-01; the user registered openpolis.space. A city builder open to everyone in the browser; "-polis" is the genre's classic naming shape without borrowing anyone's trademark (no "Sim", "SimCity", "Skylines"). Player-visible text uses the name; internal identifiers (browser storage keys `citybuilder.*`, the `citybuilder` IndexedDB and Postgres database) keep their old names so existing saves still load. The repo folder stays `simcity-web`.

## Accepted

- **D-107 — Reconcile adults, employment and pension funding.** 2026-11-12.
  The budget distinguishes all working-age adults from people employed in job buildings, unemployed adults and those unavailable because of sickness or imprisonment. Only employed adults contribute wage income tax and wage-funded bill payments. The monthly forecast groups revenues and costs and shows their separate totals before net result.

- **D-106 — Show the company estimate after tax.** 2026-11-12.
  The enterprise slider presents gross estimated turnover, the public tax receipt and turnover remaining after tax. The last amount is a transparent estimate before other business costs and changes directly with the rate.

- **D-105 — One local executable name.** 2026-11-12.
  `Compilar.bat` is the Windows build entry point. It builds the local web assets with Bun and compiles directly to `Iniciar.exe`; a later successful run replaces that file. The older PowerShell entry point delegates to the batch file, and the generated executable is ignored by Git.

- **D-104 — Wage income tax funds the general budget.** 2026-11-12.
  An independent 0–100% rate starts at 0%, is withheld from workers' gross wages and leaves pensions exempt. The tax enters resident revenue and reduces disposable income and commercial demand. The budget compares its receipts with pension expense without reserving them in a separate fund. Older saves load at 0%.

- **D-103 — Show public balance for every household bill.** 2026-11-12.
  The budget splits each bill into nominal per-resident charges, money actually collected, attributable upkeep and net balance. Energy and water base charges fund the government; rent and transport base charges remain private, while their taxes are public. Transport upkeep includes stations and rails, classified separately from roads and parks. Unserved homes and limited income reduce collections.

- **D-102 — Divide residents among overlapping public services.** 2026-11-12.
  Each police, fire, clinic, school, hospital and university keeps its own fixed upkeep. Its population-related upkeep uses only occupied homes in reach, splitting each home's residents equally among working units of the same service. This avoids billing the same residents twice for overlapping coverage; hospitals and trauma centres share one service pool.

- **D-073 — Wealth is an evolution gate, not a separate placement tool.** 2026-11-01.
  Villas, premium apartments and investor offices emerge from successful zoning only after
  the city has strong occupancy, happiness and job coverage. This preserves the existing
  simulation loop while making high-end development feel earned.

- **D-072 — Derived road-following power corridors.** 2026-11-01. Substations gate the
  advanced power network without adding a persistent wire layer: road islands and
  substation reach are deterministic from the existing world. Cities without substations
  retain legacy delivery for save compatibility.

- **D-071 — Civic leisure landmarks stay in the parks group.** 2026-11-01. Parks and shopping complexes use the existing civic placement path and Parks & Places picker. This keeps placement, upkeep, happiness and save compatibility stable while giving the city visually distinct leisure destinations; shopping zoning remains separate for demand balance.

- **D-002 — Isometric 2:1 view.** 2026-10-31. Looks much richer than top-down and fits the "very cool UI" goal.
- **D-003 — Map rotation deferred until after Phase 5.** 2026-10-31. Rotation means every building needs 4 views, which doubles the art and sorting work. *Superseded by D-032: built early at the user's request, while the renderer is still small.*
- **D-010 — TypeScript + Vite + PixiJS v8 + React HUD.** 2026-10-31. Pixi gives batched WebGL sprite rendering for smooth large maps, and React suits a rich, animated HUD. They are kept in separate layers.
- **D-011 — All art generated procedurally in code.** 2026-10-31. Original, deterministic assets with no licensing issues and a tiny download. We can swap in hand-drawn art later if we want.
- **D-012 — Struct-of-arrays world state (typed arrays).** 2026-10-31. Fast, cache-friendly, trivially serializable, and Worker-ready.
- **D-013 — Flat terrain first, elevation in Phase 6.** 2026-10-31. Slopes need many extra tile shapes and complicate roads and buildings, so we get the core loop right first.
- **D-014 — All mutations through commands.** 2026-10-31. One path for validation and cost, and it enables undo/redo later.
- **D-015 — Jotai for UI state (instead of Zustand).** 2026-10-31. User preference. Atomic state means a HUD widget only re-renders when its own atom changes. The game never imports Jotai directly: it emits events, and `app/bridge.ts` writes them into atoms.
- **D-016 — WebGL renderer for now.** 2026-10-31. Pixi's WebGPU backend is still less battle-tested, and WebGL lets the debug overlay count draw calls. We can revisit in Phase 12.
- **D-017 — Ground tiles as plain sprites in per-chunk render groups (no bake-to-texture).** 2026-10-31. Baking chunks into textures costs too much GPU memory at high zoom (≈2 MB per chunk at 1×, so hundreds of MB on large maps). One atlas texture plus Pixi v8 render groups gives near-zero per-frame cost for static chunks.
- **D-018 — One terrain first: River Valley.** 2026-10-31. User call: perfect one terrain before adding presets or the New Game screen. River Valley covers every ground type (water, shore, sand, grass, trees), so the art and transition systems get fully exercised.
- **D-019 — Dual-grid terrain rendering.** 2026-10-31. Ground is drawn on a half-tile-offset grid. Each cell's corners are 4 tile centres, and transitions use rounded marching-squares masks (14 cases × 3 wobble variants per terrain). The first try, priority overlays per tile, couldn't round the higher terrain's corners, so edges stayed staircase-shaped. Bases and overlays are drawn in two passes so overdraw never shows as seams.
- **D-020 — Pixel-art style from the user's reference image.** 2026-10-31. Bright saturated palette, crisp 1px marks, dark outlines, chunky pines, a surf band on shores. Kept in our isometric view. Reference map: `?seed=42`.
- **D-021 — Decorations as a world layer (`World.decor`).** 2026-10-31. Rocks, boulders, flowers and tall grass are visual only and get cleared when something is built. Rocks are placed where they look natural: rocky meadows, forest edges and riverbanks.
- **D-022 — Roads auto-clear trees and rocks on their path.** 2026-10-31. The clearing cost is added to the road price, and the Clear tool handles bulk clearing. It's a one-line switch in `sim/commands.ts` (`planRoad`) if we'd rather require manual clearing first.
- **D-023 — Commands live in `sim/commands.ts` as pure plan/apply pairs.** 2026-10-31. Moved from the planned `game/commands.ts` so the rules are unit-testable without the renderer. The same `plan*` function drives the tool preview and the actual build, so the preview never lies.
- **D-024 — Seed 42 is the default map.** *(Superseded by D-038.)* 2026-10-31. Without `?seed=` the game opens the approved River Valley. `N` still rolls new maps.
- **D-025 — Bridges are automatic, straight and land-to-land.** 2026-10-31. Any road or highway path that crosses water becomes a bridge, with no extra tool. Straight spans of up to 12 tiles keep the art simple (two orientations per type). They also avoid nonsense like bridges that turn or stop mid-river. Bridges store their axis and connect only at their ends.
- **D-026 — Sound is synthesised (Web Audio), not sampled.** 2026-10-31. This matches the procedural-art approach: no asset files, tiny download, and easy to tune in code. The AudioContext starts on the first user gesture. Mute persists in localStorage.
- **D-027 — Phase 4 split into 4a catalog & art, 4b zoning & growth, 4c moving cars.** 2026-10-31. User priority: see all the essential building types first. Growth and traffic build on the same catalog.
- **D-028 — Building definitions in sim, building art in render.** 2026-10-31. `sim/buildings/catalog.ts` holds gameplay data (size, level, cost, capacity) and derives the `BuildingId` union. `render/atlas/buildingArt.ts` holds a recipe per id (floors, roof, facade, extras), turned into pixels by one shared iso-box painter. The same painter draws the picker thumbnails.
- **D-029 — Simple, readable economy first.** 2026-10-31. Homes fill toward a target set by job cover (45% with no jobs, 100% with full cover), and jobs take workers in proportion to capacity. Taxes per resident and worker, upkeep per road tile. It's easy to reason about and tune now; land value, happiness and services refine it in Phases 7–9.
- **D-030 — Agents are simulated in sim/, drawn in render/.** 2026-10-31. `Traffic` holds plain agent data (path, segment, progress) and `agentPose()` computes position and heading, including right-hand lane offsets. The renderer only maps poses to pooled sprites. Agents move per frame with dt × speed (smooth), while population and budget step daily on the fixed tick.
- **D-031 — Agents draw under the objects layer.** 2026-10-31. Agents are depth-sorted among themselves only. Buildings and trees in front of a road then correctly hide cars behind them, with no global sort of every sprite each frame.
- **D-032 — View rotation through a rotated view copy of the world.** 2026-10-31.
  - The simulation, tools and traffic always work in world tiles. `ViewTransform` maps world to view for 4 quarter turns: points, tiles, rectangles, headings, connection masks and bridge axes. `ViewWorld` is the world's tile layers re-laid out in view space, re-synced after each edit (typed-array copies, well under 1 ms).
  - The chunk, road, bridge and dual-grid renderers draw that copy unchanged.
  - Overlays, effects, agents and picking convert through a shared `viewRef`.
  - On rotation the terrain is rebuilt (about 20 ms on 128×128), the camera keeps the same world point centred, and the view fades in.
  - Trees and rocks hash their *world* tile, so they look the same from every side.
- **D-033 — Buildings painted in 4 orientations from one recipe.** 2026-10-31.
  - Recipes stay in local space (front = +v). The painter rotates the layout into the view and draws whatever walls face the camera, lit from the same side every time.
  - Front-only details (door, shopfront, sign, awning, porch, canopy, parking, garden path) appear only when the front faces the camera, so rotating shows real backs. Balconies go on every side so tall homes read the same from any angle.
  - The atlas now splits into ≤4096 px pages (`AtlasBuilder`) to fit the 204 building frames.
- **D-034 — Saving: Express + SQLite backend, email/password accounts, seed-plus-changes save format.** 2026-10-31. Agreed now, built later (after Phase 4b).
  - The server is Express 5 in TypeScript. Request bodies are validated with zod. Saves are stored as a JSON blob in one SQLite `saves` table that also has metadata columns. This gives atomic writes and a straightforward move to Postgres.
  - Accounts use email and password, chosen by the user over anonymous device ids.
  - A save holds only the seed, the generator version, `funds`/`day`, the `road`/`bridge`/`zone` layers plus a cleared mask for `tree`/`decor` (base64 run-length-encoded typed arrays), and buildings as `{ def, x, y, variant }`. Terrain is regenerated from the seed, and occupants, entrances and traffic are rebuilt on load. Every save carries a version number so old saves can be migrated.
  - Buildings also need `grown`, `construction` and `fullDays` (added in 4b) in the save.
  - Pure `serialize`/`deserialize` live in `sim/save.ts`. The client goes through a `SaveStore` interface: IndexedDB for autosave, HTTP for the server.
  - We rejected replaying a command log. Any change to the rules would silently alter old cities.
- **D-035 — Growth driven by capacity-based R/C/I demand.** 2026-10-31. Demand compares *built* capacity, construction sites included, with what the other zones imply: homes need jobs, jobs follow homes, plus a small seed so a new town starts. Counting sites stops growth overshooting. Using capacity rather than occupants avoids the stall where homes that aren't yet full hold back jobs. Land value, services and pollution refine this in Phases 7–9.
- **D-036 — Road access reaches 3 tiles.** 2026-10-31. Zones are usually painted 2–3 tiles deep. A touching-only rule would leave the back rows empty forever. One rule applies to grown and placed buildings alike, and traffic uses the nearest road tile as the entrance.
- **D-037 — The Build tool still places zoned buildings (sandbox).** *(Superseded by D-042.)* 2026-10-31. Placed buildings clear the zone under them and never level up, so they don't interfere with growth. We may restrict them to civic buildings later.
- **D-038 — The New Game screen is the entry point; game options live in the URL.** 2026-10-31. This replaces D-024. With no `?seed` the app opens the New Game screen, with River Valley seed 42 (the approved map) preselected. Once a city starts, its preset, seed, size, changed sliders and name go into the URL, so a reload reopens the same city until saves exist (D-034).
- **D-039 — A time-of-day clock separate from the calendar.** 2026-10-31. Calendar days are 2 s, which is right for the economy but would make day/night strobe. A clock day lasts 90 sim seconds and drives the night tint, rush hours and ambience. The tint is a multiply quad over the world: one draw call, no filter render-to-texture.
- **D-040 — Queuing by lane gaps and junction boxes, with patience.** *(Junction boxes replaced by traffic lights, D-059.)* 2026-10-31. Cars keep a gap within their lane (one direction of one segment) and to the tail of the next lane. A junction box is used by one movement at a time, except straight traffic on the same axis. Waiting 4 s overrides the box and waiting 25 s despawns, so the network can never deadlock. Traffic lights can come later on top of the boxes.
- **D-041 — Bus stops are automatic.** 2026-10-31. There's no stop tool yet. Stops go outside the busiest buildings on straight road, spaced 6 tiles apart. Buses run stop to stop and dwell 2 s. Shelters are drawn in the agent layer, so they depth-sort with the cars.
- **D-042 — The Build tool places civic buildings only.** 2026-10-31. User decision. Homes, shops and industry only appear through zoning, which keeps the growth rules meaningful. `PLACEABLE_IDS` in the catalog drives the picker and `Game.setBuilding`. Sim-level `planPlace` still accepts any building, since tests and future scenarios use it.
- **D-043 — Heights live on tile centres, slopes are capped at one level per tile, and a level is 8 px.** 2026-10-31.
  - Tile-centre heights fit the dual grid (D-019): each ground cell's corners are tile centres, so cells become sloped quads with no new data.
  - With an 8 px step, under `HALF_H`, the terrain can never hide anything behind it, so the layered drawing order (ground → roads → agents → objects) still holds with no depth buffer.
  - Water is pinned to level 0, which keeps shores and bridges gentle.
- **D-044 — Ground drawn as merged meshes with a small custom shader.** 2026-10-31.
  - Sprites can't bend to slopes. Each chunk now has one mesh per atlas page, built from the same atlas frames, with per-vertex colour (hill light, zone tint) and fog.
  - Water animates by rewriting its UVs in visible chunks.
  - Roads, zone lots, the grid, hover and previews use the same bent tile quads. Taller things (trees, buildings, bridges, agents) stay sprites, lifted by height.
  - Shaders are cached per atlas page and released before the pages are destroyed.
- **D-045 — Borderlands are render-only and replace the soil slab.** 2026-10-31. User choice. An extended world (map + ring, `sim/terrain/borderlands.ts`) copies the map into its middle, so the seam matches. It continues edge water outward and rises into hills and mountains with fog. Nothing in the sim reads it. Tree sprites only within 10 tiles; further out, forest is tinted ground, which keeps the sprite count low. It's rebuilt when the map edge is terraformed. The camera may pan a quarter of the ring past the edge.
- **D-046 — Borderlands simplified to low land fading into fog.** 2026-10-31. User feedback: the hill-and-mountain ring looked smeared, because steep slopes squash and stretch tile textures in this view and the hill light became big bands. The ring now eases down from the map edge to low rolling ground, with no mountains or rock peaks, and the fog starts sooner. This revises D-045.
- **D-047 — No hill shading, and a zoom-out limit set by the map.** 2026-10-31. User feedback.
  - Slope lighting showed as dark blotches on gentle hills, so ground, roads, the ring and the minimap all use plain colours. The ring's dark forest tint went too; tree sprites now reach 20 tiles out instead.
  - The furthest zoom-out is now `(screenW + 2·screenH) ÷ mapWidthPx` instead of a fixed 0.2×. That's the zoom at which the screen just fits inside the map diamond, never below 0.2× or above 1×. It's recomputed on resize.
- **D-048 — Zoning explains itself.** 2026-10-31. User feedback: building houses was confusing. The Build button only offered parks, and zones far from roads silently never grew. Build is renamed Civic and links to the zone tools. Zone tiles out of road reach show amber while dragging and grey once zoned, the cost label counts them, and a toast explains.
- **D-049 — Starting funds are 700,000,000 during development.** 2026-10-31. User request, to test freely. `BALANCE.startingFunds` is marked as a development value and should go back to a real budget (20,000 before) before release. `?funds=N` still overrides it.
- **D-050 — Level flattens to the area's average.** 2026-10-31. User decision. Dragging with Level makes the whole rectangle one height: the rounded average of its movable tiles, recomputed live as the drag grows. Before, it used the start tile's height, which surprised people when they started a drag on a slope.
- **D-051 — Flat land, no terraforming, and no Dezone tool.** 2026-10-31. User decision: hills and the Raise/Lower/Level tools added complexity the game doesn't need. This replaces D-050.
  - Every map is flat. The Hills slider and per-preset hills are gone, and so are the terraform tools and `sim/terraform.ts`. The borderlands ring is flat too.
  - The height-aware rendering (sloped meshes, lifting, picking) stays in place but sees only level 0. It costs nothing, and it keeps the door open if elevation ever comes back.
  - Dezone was hard to understand. **Clear** is now the one removal tool: it removes trees, rocks, roads, buildings *and zones*. A bulldozed grown building therefore doesn't regrow; to rebuild, zone the land again.
- **D-052 — Utilities travel over roads; no pipes or power lines.** 2026-11-01. User goal: the essentials without extra complexity. Each road island pools the capacity of the power plants and water towers on it and serves buildings in id order. Missing utilities empty a building and show a badge, with a one-time hint.
- **D-053 — Services are coverage circles that shape occupancy and growth.** *(The occupancy part is replaced by happiness, D-061.)* 2026-11-01. Police, fire, clinic and school cover a radius. Each one covering a building adds 7.5% to its occupancy (70% with none). Level 2 needs at least one service, level 3 needs all four. Fire stations also prevent random fires, which only start once the city has 200 residents.
- **D-054 — Density as separate zone tools (Homes, Apartments, Shops, Offices, Industry).** 2026-11-01. User request: a clear way to get apartments and offices. Each zoned building has a density. High-density zones start at walk-ups and small offices and grow into towers. Low-density zones top out at level 2.
- **D-055 — /demo is a planned city grown by the real sim, then built out.** 2026-11-01. User request: a seeded, beautiful city to show off. The layout is planned (grid, rings, service blocks), growth runs with the normal rules, and empty lots are filled at the end. Two years of growth alone leaves many blocks empty, because growth follows demand. Utilities are added per road island so islands cut off by the river aren't left dark.
- **D-056 — No free starter utilities.** 2026-11-01. User decision. Building the first power plant and water tower is the player's first goal; the one-time hints explain it.
- **D-057 — Next steps: systems foundation → traffic overhaul → citizen needs → backend saving.** 2026-11-01. User decision. Saving comes last because the save format (D-034) stores only the seed, layers and buildings. Traffic and needs are recomputed from those, so reworking them doesn't change the format.
- **D-058 — "Systems as services" means separate in-game modules, not network services.** 2026-11-01.
  - The sim runs 10 ticks a second, far too often to send over a network.
  - Each system (access, utilities, coverage, fire, growth, population, budget, traffic, later needs and transit) is its own module behind one `SimSystem` interface. A `Simulation` core runs them in a fixed order.
  - This keeps each system testable and replaceable, and ready to move into a Web Worker (Phase 12). The backend stays a separate small server for accounts and saves only.
- **D-059 — Traffic has two layers: a commute model, and visible cars sampled from it.** 2026-11-01. User report: cities gridlocked (48% of cars stuck on a test town). The game rules (congestion, commute time) come from a daily flow model over the road graph. The visible cars drive real commute trips, but their number is capped by road capacity (0.35 per road tile) and they only spawn where there's room, so the roads can't overfill. Traffic lights with "don't block the junction" replace the junction boxes. This approach copes with any city size and keeps the visuals honest about where traffic is heavy.
- **D-060 — City art: variants change form, furniture is a sprite per road tile, and pedestrians follow a mitred footpath route.** 2026-11-01. User request: buildings that vary like a real city, footpaths people stay on, and overhead bridges. Variants are recipes that change massing, height, roof, facade and rooftop, not just colour. Variant counts are per building and wrap with `%`, so saves stay valid. Street furniture lives in the objects layer, so it sorts with buildings; anything on the far side of a tile is kept thin, because agents draw underneath. "Overhead bridge" is built as **foot overbridges and sign gantries over highways**. True grade-separated road overpasses would change road connectivity in the sim, so they're left for a later phase. Junction lights are the live ones from the traffic sim (D-059), not furniture.
- **D-061 — One happiness number per building, from weighted needs, drives occupancy, growth and abandonment.** 2026-11-01. User request: residents should say what they need.
  - Each zoned building scores its needs: services, shops, parks, commute, clean air, jobs or workers, customers. Their weighted average is its happiness.
  - Happiness sets occupancy (55–100%), replacing the per-service bonus so services aren't counted twice, and gates level-ups.
  - Abandonment is on (user decision): 30 days below 0.3, recovering after 15 days at 0.45 or more.
  - Complaints name each unhappy building's worst need, shown as a few rotating speech bubbles plus the "Citizens say…" panel.
- **D-062 — Saves go to Postgres (Docker), and the server is built in modules with layers.** 2026-11-01. User decision; this replaces the SQLite part of D-034.
  - Postgres runs in Docker for development (`docker-compose.yml`) and is what a hosted game will use for many players. The driver is `pg`, which is pure JS. Tests use PGlite (Postgres compiled to WASM, running in-process), so `pnpm test` needs no Docker.
  - Repositories depend only on a small `Db` interface (`query`, `exec`, `transaction`). Columns stay int4, float8, text and timestamptz, so both drivers return plain JS values. Save data is JSON text that the server passes through without parsing it again.
  - Layout (user request: clean, structured backend): `config` → `db/` (driver and migrations) → `http/` (errors, validation, middleware) → `modules/<feature>/` (`*.schemas` → `*.routes` → `*.service` → `*.repository`). Routes stay thin. Services hold the rules and throw `HttpError`, and one error middleware formats the responses.
  - The server runs on Node's built-in TypeScript support (erasable syntax only: no enums or parameter properties), so it has no build step.
  - Guests save to IndexedDB. A guest city moves to the account on its first save after the player logs in.
- **D-063 — Master plan for Phases 11–22 ([05-master-plan.md](05-master-plan.md)).** 2026-11-01. User answers to its §16:
  - Residents are simulated as groups per home (age, education, wealth), with individuals sampled only for display.
  - Every new map starts with a regional highway (a causeway on islands). Highways need ramps for new links. The Web Worker move comes first (Phase 11).
  - Career mode unlocks buildings by city stage, and Sandbox opens everything. Buying map land is not planned. Road wear is an option, off by default.
  - Disasters are realistic and tied to the terrain (tsunami and cyclones on islands and coasts, floods in river valleys and lakes, wildfire in forests, tornado and drought on plains). Each has a visible risk, a warning, a physical model, mitigation and an Off/Mild/Realistic setting.
- **D-064 — Road tiles are judged one by one; rock no longer cancels the rest of a drag.** 2026-11-01. User request: a highway dragged across a rock patch failed for every tile after the rock. Rock, buildings and the map edge now block only their own tiles. A water crossing is still checked as a whole: it must be straight, at most the maximum span, start and land on buildable land, and not cross a bridge sideways. If any of that fails, the whole crossing is red, so no bridge is ever left dangling. A drag can therefore leave gaps; the player sees exactly which tiles are red. The `/demo` builder keeps the old "stop at the first blocked tile" rule (`leadingRun`), so the showcase city is unchanged; its road, bridge and building layout hashes match on seeds 42, 7 and 1234.
- **D-065 — Phase 11 order, erasable-only sim code, and exact save continuation.** 2026-11-01.
  - Phase 11 runs in six measured steps: harness and timings → history → time-slicing → fields → road graph → Worker ([specs/engine.md](specs/engine.md)). The Worker stays inside Phase 11, as D-063 decided, but comes last, because it moves everything the earlier steps build.
  - `src/sim`, `src/shared`, `scripts` and `server` use erasable TypeScript only (no parameter properties, no enums; ESLint enforces it). Node then runs the real sim with no build step: the harness now, other tools later. `tsx` was rejected because it needs esbuild's native install script, which the pnpm build-script guard blocks.
  - Saves carry the sim's day-to-day memory (random stream states, commute volume and minutes) in an optional `sim` block, and `loadSimulation` restores it, so a loaded city plays out exactly as the running one would. Old saves still load (cold). This is also how the Worker will receive the city.
- **D-066 — Tile and building art is split into small components with a modern, 3D-looking style.** 2026-11-01. User direction for all art work from now on. Each kind of tile (ground, water edge, road piece, bridge part, street furniture, building massing, roof, facade, rooftop detail) lives in its own small, clearly named module with one job, not in large mixed files (today `buildingArt.ts` and `civicArt.ts` are 800–1,000 lines). The look is elegant and modern: clear 3D form (lit top, mid and shadow faces, soft contact shadows, ambient occlusion where faces meet), a restrained, themeable palette defined as tokens rather than scattered colour literals, and switchable themes. Planned as its own art step (roadmap), with the refactor done first and the visuals changed second, so each change can be reviewed.
- **D-067 — Feature order after 11.1: energy → water, sewage and drainage → health and education → traffic graph → public transport → research.** 2026-11-01. User direction: make the game more advanced and deterministic with public transport, hospitals, universities, research, proper traffic, drainage, sewage, and more advanced water and power. Engine work now comes with the feature that needs it, instead of finishing Phase 11 first: the deterministic tick clock with energy (A), the segment graph with traffic (D). Drainage and research were added to the master plan (§12.2b, §12.4).
- **D-068 — The sim clock advances in fixed ticks.** 2026-11-01. The time of day used to advance with variable frame time, so once power depended on the hour, two machines would play the same city differently. `City.ticks` counts fixed 0.1 s ticks; `Simulation.tick()` advances it, fires `onHour` on clock-hour boundaries and runs `day()` every 60 ticks. The game, the harness and the tests all drive the sim through it; rendering only reads it.
- **D-069 — Transport is rebuilt around one road-type table, with 2-tile dual carriageways for wide roads.** 2026-11-01. User direction: fix the transport layer once and for all, with 6- and 8-lane roads, flyovers, huge bridges, public transport and varied pavements ([specs/transport.md](specs/transport.md)).
  - Every road property (lanes, capacity, speed, cost, upkeep, noise, sidewalks, frontage, art) lives in `sim/roadTypes.ts`, and no system branches on a specific road type, so adding a road is data plus one art file.
  - A tile is about 64 px across: room for 4 lanes, not 8. So 6- and 8-lane roads are 2 tiles wide (two one-way carriageways and a median), laid by one drag. That fits real road design and pairs with ramps, flyovers and interchanges.
  - Roads upgrade in place, paying the difference. Highways and expressways give no building frontage on new links.
- **D-070 — Demand v2 (first part): four bars, one shared job pool.** 2026-11-01. User report: factories said "we can't find workers" (804 workers for 1,515 jobs) while homes said "nowhere to shop". Industry demand had ignored the jobs that shops, offices and civic buildings already filled, and offices shared one bar with shops. Now offices and industry share `open jobs = workforce × 1.05 − all jobs`, and shops follow residents. - **D-071 — Strict saving: verify before writing, journal synchronously, load the newest copy.** 2026-11-01. User report: progress was lost on coming back.
  - Root cause: `parseSave` required a battery's charge to be an integer, but it is fractional, so batteries were dropped on load. `buildingTiles` then pointed past the shortened list and threw "bad building tile", so the whole city failed to open. Counters are now rounded, not grounds for dropping a building. `buildingTiles` is only a cross-check and can no longer fail a load.
  - `verifySave` runs before every write: the snapshot must survive JSON → `parseSave` with every building, every layer must decode, and every footprint must fit without overlap. A failing snapshot never overwrites a good save.
  - IndexedDB and fetch are async, and a closing tab kills them. Cloud bodies over 60 KB can't use `keepalive`. So every save first goes to a localStorage journal (synchronous), and so does `visibilitychange`/`pagehide`. An entry is cleared only after its store confirms. Loading picks the newer of the store copy and the journal (`savedAt`), and falls back to the journal if the store can't be read. A new city closed before its first save is restored into IndexedDB on the next visit. Failed saves retry after 5 s, not 45 s.
  - Saves also keep the land (`ground`, `elev` layers, a few KB), so a future terrain-generator change can't move the ground under an existing city. Older saves still regenerate it.
  - Cost: one stringify + parse + localStorage write per save (at most once a second while editing), a few ms.
